Research interests

I work on a variety of research directions on AI, AI and Law, ML, safety, alignment, technical governance and capabilities. My technical work spans structural causal models, deterministic multi-agent simulation, runtime control, and evaluation harnesses; on the legal side, I work on liability, causal attribution, and comparative regulation across the globe (expertise in the EU, UK, and China). I am currently based in Singapore.

  • Equilibrium-level harm in multi-agent systems. Harm that emerges from agent interaction even when no single agent has a privately profitable deviation, and why per-agent evaluation misses it.
  • Runtime control of informational exclusion. How congestion and shared memory displace underrepresented evidence, and whether harness-level controllers can preserve service and state coverage without knowing which source is decisive.
  • Causal attribution and responsibility. Time-unrolled structural causal models over weights, prompts, memory, and harness decisions; interventional replay and degree-of-responsibility measures with evidentiary weight in legal settings.
  • Agent memory safety and auditability. The retention → discoverability → reachability → influence ladder and forensic audit protocols.
  • Machine learning and AI. Foundations of LLMs and agents, evaluation methodology, and how emergent behaviour arises from systems-level interaction.
  • Comparative AI governance. Regulation of high-capability systems across the globe, including machine-readable regulation and LawAI Gov Hub (14,011 records, 205 jurisdictions).
